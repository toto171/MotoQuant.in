**Subject:** Physics-validated drag simulator built for the Indian scene

Hi [EDITOR NAME — verify],

Motoroids has been one of the more performance-focused independent moto blogs in India for a long time — the coverage of the H2/H2R, the Panigale V4 R reviews, and the recurring drag-day write-ups out of MMRT and Kari are exactly the editorial slant that suggested this might be a fit.

I'm Hritwik Mudhai, the developer of MotoQuant Pro — a quarter-mile drag simulator built on a first-principles physics engine, developed in Pune over the last 14 months. Short summary of where it stands:

- **336 bikes catalogued** including the full Indian sport/commuter lineup (Pulsar NS200/NS400Z/N250, Apache RR 310 / RTR 200, KTM Duke 200/250/390 + RC 200/390, R15 V1-V4, RE GT 650 / Interceptor 650, Triumph Speed 400, Hero Xtreme 200S 4V) plus the international litre-sport and 600cc fleet, plus 20 two-stroke bikes (RD350, NSR250R, RGV250, KTM 300 EXC, Yezdi 250 — the actual Indian drag-scene 2T fleet).
- **15 physics sub-models** — engine with dyno-curve injection where available (25 of 336 have real curves, the rest synthetic), Pacejka tire model with thermal coefficients across 232 tires, aero with rider-tuck transitions, launch dynamics with two-step and slipper-clutch slip-phase modelling.
- **79 published benchmarks** validated against. Reference baselines (GSX-R 1000 K5 at 10.0334s, R1 4C8 at 10.2859s, Hayabusa Gen-1 at 10.4712s) pinned to four decimals as a regression contract. Top-10 best-matched bikes hold a mean error of ±0.032s. The litre-sport ≥195hp cluster sits at ±0.16s mean absolute error; the 200-300cc Indian commuter cluster at ±1.4s with full disclosure on why (data-quality bottleneck on stock-bike timeslips, not model-quality).
- **Built specifically for the Indian drag scene** — venues include Aamby Valley, MMRT, Kari, Hyderabad with 12-month seasonal density-altitude data; pricing is in INR; Indian retailer pricing for 562 catalogued performance parts.

The editorial angle, if it's a fit: this is the first tool of its kind I'm aware of that's been built in India, for the Indian drag scene, with a transparent validation methodology and per-cluster bias disclosure. There's a story in either the technical methodology piece (how do you simulate a quarter-mile in 15 sub-models?), the validation piece (where does the simulator agree with magazine tests, and where does it diverge, and why?), or the practical-tooling piece (Pune tuning shops using it for customer-facing ET predictions on Akrapovic-plus-ECU builds).

Would a press kit or a 30-minute walkthrough be useful? Happy to send the validation report, sample sim outputs for any specific bike you'd want to see, or hop on a call.
